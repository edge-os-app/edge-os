For example, 13 / 21 means 13 times with the rise and 21 without. A positive difference means the first group did better on average. A negative difference means it did worse. Differences are before costs, with an assumed three-minute data delay.

The last result assumes a cost of 0.5% to buy and 0.5% to sell. These costs were assumed, not measured from real trades.

Why the early results were not enough
  • The later two-day period had at most 48 hourly opportunities. The agreed test needed at least 30 with the rise and 30 without. It could not meet that requirement, which was kept unchanged.
  • Versions 1 and 3 looked better in the first period but worse in the later period. Versions 2 and 4 were slightly better before costs in the later period, with too few examples to support choosing either.
  • All four later averages were negative after either assumed cost: 0.5% or 1% for each buy and sale.
  • Much of the early gain came from September 25. All four versions had a negative total price result on September 27. The middle result in both 15-minute versions was also negative in the first period.
How the study was done
  1. Explore the idea using September 25 to 28 prices.
  2. Check the same rules again on September 28 to 30 prices.
  3. Keep September 30 to October 2 data unopened for an independent final check. No supported strategy was selected to use it.

We compare times with the required rise against times without it. For a rule requiring more than a 1% rise, a 2% rise qualifies; a 0.5% rise does not. These are explanations of the rule, not added study results.

Then we compare the price changes over the next 30 minutes. The calculations do not show actual trading profits.

Data sources, missing prices and cost assumptions
Source
Recorded MOE/WMON study using 26,146 decoded buys and sales, collected October 3. Choosing a currently active market can overlook markets that failed or disappeared. Complete archive coverage and when trades became final remain unverified.
Missing prices
First period: 34 of 72 opportunities usable. 24 entry prices and 20 exit prices were missing, with 6 missing both. Later period: 47 usable outcomes and 1 missing entry. A missing exit blocks later paper entries until it is resolved.
When data could be used
An assumed 180-second delay starts after the trade or price interval ends, whichever is later. Original delivery and finality times are unknown. A 60-second delay gave the same results, but that is not an independent repeat.
Longer delay
At a 600-second delay, every opportunity failed because the information would arrive after the decision. These rejected cases do not count as zero returns.
Costs included
Assumed costs of 0%, 0.5% or 1% for each buy and sale. Actual fees were not measured.
Costs left out
Token transfer charges and balance changes, wrapping, trade routing, MON network fees, price impact and how much could actually be traded. The study cannot establish real profitability.
Rules and research record
Four rule templates were checked and reloaded using a test compiler. They are not deployable MOE strategies. The recorded study used one hypothesis, four proposals and 24 evaluations, including two real Hermes conversations. No live conversation runs here.
